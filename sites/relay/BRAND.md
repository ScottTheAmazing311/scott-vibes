# RELAY — Brand Book v1
Commercial moving & logistics for office environments: decommission, transport, storage, reassembly.

## Name
**Relay** (full legal style: *Relay Workplace Logistics*).
A relay is a handoff executed at speed without dropping anything — the entire value
proposition in one word. Short, spellable, verbs well ("Relay your office").

Alternates considered (keep for trademark/domain fallback):
- **Second Shift Logistics** — office moves happen after hours; blue-collar pride.
- **Cadence Commercial** — rhythm, schedule-keeping.
- **Dock & Day** — the move weekend in two words.

## Tagline
**"Out Friday. Open Monday."**
The only metric a facilities director actually cares about is downtime. The tagline is
a promise with a deadline in it. Secondary line for long formats: *"Like nothing moved
but the address."*

## Positioning
For operations and facilities leaders moving 50–1,000 employees, Relay is the moving
partner that treats an office move as a logistics problem, not a trucking job —
inventoried, barcoded, insured, and timed to the business week. Unlike household movers
who "also do offices," Relay only does commercial, so buildings, landlords, and IT
closets hold no surprises.

## Audience
- Facilities / workplace operations managers (the buyer)
- Office managers and COOs at 50–500 person companies
- Commercial property managers & tenant-rep brokers (the referrers)

## Voice
Plain, punctual, numerate. Short sentences. Specifics over adjectives: say
"COI to your building within the hour," not "fully insured and professional."
Never exclamation points. The brand sounds like a crew lead who has done this
four hundred times and is not worried.

## Messaging pillars
1. **Zero lost workdays** — moves land on weekends and nights; Monday is sacred.
2. **Chain of custody** — every asset tagged, scanned at dock-out and dock-in.
3. **Building-ready** — COI, masonite, elevator pads, freight reservations handled.
4. **Donate-first decommission** — furniture routed to donation and recycling before
   landfill; tonnage reported back to the client.

## Color
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Signal Amber | `#F2A900` | The accent. CTAs, the logo badge, highlights. Hazard-vest amber: visibility, work-site vernacular. |
| Dock Slate | `#16202B` | Primary ink. Headlines, body on light, text on amber. |
| Concrete | `#F1F2EF` | Light ground. Cool, warehouse-floor neutral (not cream). |
| Night Dock | `#10181F` | Dark ground. Hero bands, dark theme base. |
| Steel | `#44515E` | Secondary text, captions. |
| Clear Green | `#2E7D4F` | Semantic only: "on-time," "open," confirmations. |

Rule: amber is the only brand accent. Green is status, never decoration.

## Typography
- **Display: Barlow Condensed 600/700, uppercase** — DIN-adjacent, highway-and-freight
  signage DNA. Tight leading, slight tracking.
- **Body: Barlow 400/500/600** — same superfamily, reads clean at paragraph sizes.
- **Data/manifest: IBM Plex Mono 400/500** — inventory tags, manifests, numbers, labels.
All on Google Fonts. Fallbacks: Arial Narrow / Helvetica / monospace respectively.

## Logo
Files in this folder:
- `logo-mark.svg` — the badge: amber rounded square, double chevron in Dock Slate.
  The two chevrons are the handoff — one motion passed to the next. Reads at 16px.
- `logo-lockup.svg` — mark + RELAY wordmark + "Workplace Logistics" for light grounds.
- `logo-lockup-dark.svg` — same for dark grounds.

Usage: mark alone for favicons/avatars/truck doors at small scale; lockup elsewhere.
Clear space = the width of one chevron. Never recolor the chevrons; on amber they are
always Dock Slate. Note: lockup text uses SVG `<text>` (Barlow Condensed); before
production print/signage use, convert text to outlines in a vector editor.
