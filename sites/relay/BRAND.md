# RELAY — Brand Book v2
Commercial moving & logistics for office environments: decommission, transport, storage, reassembly.

v2 note: the v1 identity leaned on a dark "spec sheet" treatment (mono tags, condensed
uppercase, dark ground with a single accent) that read as templated. v2 repositions the
visual identity as an established company with a print heritage: light ground, slab-serif
headings in sentence case, plain informative copy, subject-matter illustration (floor
plans) instead of decorative texture.

## Name
**Relay** (full legal style: *Relay Workplace Logistics*).
A relay is a handoff executed at speed without dropping anything. Short, spellable,
verbs well ("Relay your office").

Alternates considered (keep for trademark/domain fallback):
- **Second Shift Logistics** — office moves happen after hours; blue-collar pride.
- **Cadence Commercial** — rhythm, schedule-keeping.
- **Dock & Day** — the move weekend in two words.

## Tagline
**"Out Friday. Open Monday."**
The only metric a facilities director cares about is downtime; the tagline is a promise
with a deadline in it. Used sparingly: once in hero body copy, once in the footer,
set in italic slab. It is a signature, never a shout.

## Positioning
For operations and facilities leaders moving 50 to 1,000 employees, Relay treats an
office move as a scheduling and inventory problem with trucks attached. Everything the
brand says should sound like it was written by the crew lead who has done this four
hundred times: specific, calm, numerate.

## Audience
- Facilities / workplace operations managers (the buyer)
- Office managers and COOs at 50–500 person companies
- Commercial property managers & tenant-rep brokers (the referrers)

## Voice
Plain, punctual, numerate. Sentence case everywhere, including headings. Specifics over
adjectives: "COI to your building within the hour," never "fully insured and
professional." No exclamation points, no slogan-fragments as section titles, no
"not X, but Y" constructions. Prefer a table or a list to a claim.

## Color
| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Signal Amber | `#F2A900` | Brand accent: logo chevrons, CTAs, highlights. The color of moving trucks and work gloves. |
| Slate | `#232B32` | Primary text; footer ground. |
| Blanket Blue | `#44607A` | Secondary surface (the quilted moving-blanket color). Solid bands, illustration fills. |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Primary ground. |
| Warm Grey | `#F4F4F1` | Alternating section band. |
| Clear Green | `#2E7D4F` | Semantic only: confirmations, "on time." |

Rules: light ground is the default; amber appears on white or slate, never floods a page.
Blue is the only large colored surface. Green is status, never decoration.

## Typography
- **Headings: Bitter (slab serif), 600/700, sentence case.** Slab serifs are freight and
  van-line heritage; set at modest sizes, never viewport-filling.
- **Body & UI: Source Sans 3, 400/600.**
- No monospace in brand materials; manifests and barcodes are operations, not decoration.
Fallbacks: Georgia / system sans.

## Logo
Files in this folder:
- `logo-lockup.svg` — amber double chevron plus "Relay" wordmark (Bitter 700) and
  "Workplace Logistics" in small caps-style sans. For light grounds.
- `logo-lockup-dark.svg` — same for dark grounds (slate footer, vehicle wraps on dark).
- `logo-mark.svg` — avatar/favicon version: slate square (4px corner), amber chevrons.

The two chevrons are the handoff, one motion passed to the next. Clear space equals one
chevron width. Chevrons are always Signal Amber; the wordmark takes the text color of
its ground. Lockup text uses SVG `<text>`; convert to outlines before production
print or signage use.
